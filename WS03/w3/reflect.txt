🗎 why static member functions cannot be querries
When a non-static member function is declared with the const qualifier, it means that the function does not modify the state of the object. The const qualifier applies to the implicit "this" pointer, which points to the instance of the class.
Static member functions do not have a "this" pointer, and they cannot be made const because they do not operate on an instance of the class. Instead, they operate on the class itself
